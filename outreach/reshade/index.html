1. Don't use AI — and why

Begin with conventional tools and human inquiry. A language model is not a compiler, a reproducible experiment, proof of correctness, or permission to extract another person's ideas.

  • A compiler, linter, debugger, numeric solver, published specification, or test harness may answer the question more reliably.
  • Model output can sound certain while misrepresenting code or its author's intentions.
  • Sending third-party code or logs to a service can create confidentiality, license, consent, and provenance problems.
  • Author-identifying methodological fingerprint extraction and archival require separate ethical consideration and are prohibited without specific applicable consent under this proposed doctrine.
  • If a creator has expressed an objection to the intended machine-assisted use, respect the boundary.

2. Do this instead

  1. Define the research question, required behavior, invariants, and failure modes first.
  2. Study authorized public shading theory, mathematics, APIs, and documentation.
  3. Ask the creator directly when appropriate and welcome. Silence is not consent.
  4. Use deterministic compilers, linting, numerical references, static checks, and reproducible differential tests where permitted.
  5. Construct your own controlled experimental shader if you need to observe a development process and cannot permissibly study another person's source.
  6. Keep records of sources, human decisions, revisions, test results, corrections, and uncertainty.

3. If you use AI, these are our objections

We object to treating another person's source or distinctive intellectual method as unrestricted extraction material. We also object to unverifiable model output replacing engineering evidence.

4. Decomposting is not extractive mining

Extractive mining, in this ethical context, means stripping a work or creator's methods for advantage without sufficient permission, provenance, or contribution. Decomposting describes the intended regenerative cycle:

Decompose → Study → Reason → Learn → Respect provenance → Correct → Regrow.

One conceptual separation: hermetically seal Code A → identify independently corroborated, permitted Pattern B → disallow Code A − Pattern B.

Code A is protected source; Pattern B is legitimately transferable general knowledge; Code A − Pattern B signifies excluded, source-specific residue—not an instruction to compute or archive it. If the research cannot establish Pattern B without crossing an author's boundary, stop or use independent specifications or a consenting reference.

5. A case study: creating shaders to study development

My research began with questions about error propagation, verification, and development methodology. ReShade and general shading knowledge provided a useful domain, but documentation and observations of the development process were uneven. I therefore created experimental shaders within a human-directed hybrid development workflow to generate my own inspectable research history.

These shaders were experimental apparatus as well as outputs. The objective was to understand how errors arise and are corrected, not to infer another programmer's complete intentions or archive a personal coding fingerprint.

My depth-oracle decisions offer a case for criticism. The self-reported errata, Record 02, documents regrets about research direction, considering existing permissible work, and an unsuccessful community opening. These statements do not attest to anyone else's intentions.

6. Your place in this ecosystem

Developers, shader authors, skeptical researchers, users, and reviewers are invited to voluntarily criticize the guidance and my own documented methods. No person is asked to approve AI, surrender source, or endorse this project.

A private, redacted human–AI conversation may be offered to a willing reviewer under a separately agreed sharing scope. No such link is created or published here. Declining or not responding is sufficient and requires no explanation.

Small, swappable research modules for documentation, verification, and attestation are an intended non-displacing architecture, not a demonstrated guarantee of zero impact on gaming or LLM ecosystems.
