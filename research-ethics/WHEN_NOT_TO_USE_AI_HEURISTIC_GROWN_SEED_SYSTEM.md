# When Not to Use AI in Research: Heuristic-Grown Seeds and Factories of Factories of Factories

**Authorial position:** Alia-June (Michael Stuart)  
**Publication date:** 2026-11-10  
**Status:** Personal research position and architectural proposal. Not a claim of completed implementation, independent attestation, or formal proof.  
**Editorial provenance:** Developed from the author's stated research position with AI assistance for drafting and organization. The named projects and architectural aims are not represented as AI-originated inventions.

## Why the question matters to me

The question “When should we not use AI in research?” cannot be answered solely by examining the capability of a language model. We must also examine the researcher’s circumstances, the nature of the work, the methods available for verification, and the dependencies that the chosen tools introduce.

Much of my systems research has been carried out alone, sometimes entirely in my head, under substantial limitations of resources. Mental illness and periods of hospitalization have interrupted my ability to organize, preserve, and continue that research. The breadth of work I have undertaken would ordinarily be distributed among researchers, programmers, reviewers, and other specialists.

AI has sometimes assisted with documentation, organization, comparison, formulation, and the exploration of alternatives. It has helped compensate for the absence of a team. That is a practical account of assistance, not an argument that generated answers are necessarily correct, nor a surrender of authorship, judgment, or authority.

The ideas and research questions precede that assistance.

## The long-standing research concern

I remember AT&T advertisements in the 1990s presenting a future of connectivity and paid services. Those presentations led me to consider the possible risks of technological dependency and to begin formulating ways to retain independent means of construction.

This is a personal recollection of the origins of a research concern, not evidence that a particular later outcome was predicted.

The central question has endured: how can independent functional capacity be established, preserved, reconstructed, and extended without depending permanently on a particular service, vendor, or opaque construction mechanism?

## Factories of factories of factories

> I do not build cars. I do not build factories that build cars. I build factories of factories of factories.

This is a statement of the scale of my research, not a claim that such a system has already been achieved.

An application is a finished artifact. A factory can produce applications or other artifacts. A factory-construction system can produce factories. My concern extends to the means by which **factory-construction systems themselves** may be constructed, revised, tested, reproduced, and replaced.

The aim is recursive **construction capability**, not merely large-scale production and not unlimited self-replication. Each new level of construction brings questions of function, dependencies, authority, resource consumption, error propagation, and validation.

## Heuristic-grown seeds, rather than an AI-grown system

I propose a **heuristic-grown seed system** in place of a system whose development depends primarily on generative AI.

A seed is a candidate functional constituent. Its admission into construction requires an account of what its base functionality actually is, how that functionality is known, where it came from, what may be done with it, and what constraints govern its composition.

I distinguish three starting sources:

1. **Home-grown seeds** — functional constituents independently constructed for the research.
2. **Freely obtained seeds** — constituents lawfully available for the intended examination, use, or transformation, within their applicable terms.
3. **Purchased seeds** — constituents obtained through purchase where the actual rights and source access necessary for the intended construction are provided.

Merely possessing or accessing code is not permission for every use. Source access, licensing, consent, attribution, technical suitability, and validation must remain separate questions.

**Fundamental functionality must be declared.** It must be possible to distinguish what a seed promises from what it has actually demonstrated. A composition that succeeds syntactically is not necessarily semantically correct; a preserved copy is not automatically authentic, authorized, or suitable for a new context.

A heuristic-grown process would guide the selection, decomposition, adaptation, composition, inspection, testing, and revision of those seeds through explicit, inspectable criteria. Heuristics are not proofs. They provide reviewable decision methods whose outcomes require the applicable independent checks.

These are **initial variants for research**, not proven equivalent implementations, activated integrations, or assertions that either presently constructs factories of factories of factories. Their relationships and admissible roles require separate technical specifications and validation.

## Asymmetric growth and resilience

My objective is **asymmetric growth**: a comparatively small, declared, inspectable set of foundational mechanisms supporting the construction of a much wider range of functional systems and, in turn, construction systems.

Resilience is equally important. The intended architecture should make it possible to understand what was constructed, preserve the necessary evidence, recover from interruption or loss, and reconstruct capability when dependencies are no longer available.

Neither asymmetry nor resilience follows automatically from recursive generation. A factory can reproduce mistakes. A seed can embed hidden assumptions. A heuristic can select a bad design. A test can be too narrow. A new composition may invalidate guarantees established for its parts.

These are research obligations. The actual growth advantage, recoverability, correctness, and operational boundaries must be measured, demonstrated, and independently examined where necessary.

## When I would not use AI

I would not treat an AI answer as a substitute for an available, directly applicable specification, compiler, debugger, mathematical derivation, controlled experiment, reproducible benchmark, or properly authorized human review.

I would not use AI-generated output to imply independent derivation, consent, provenance, execution, correctness, or attestation that the evidence does not support. I would not treat access to another person's work as unrestricted permission to extract, model, or reuse their intellectual methodology.

Where AI is useful, its role must be bounded and described: what it received, what it generated or transformed, what was adopted, what was rejected, what was verified, and what remains unresolved.

The point is not that AI must never participate. The point is that **a researcher's independent means of inquiry and construction should not depend on AI where inspectable, independently verifiable means can be established**.

## The proposition

I am not primarily trying to have AI build software for me.

I am researching how to establish the foundations from which **factories of factories of factories** can be built through declared functionality, heuristic-guided construction, controlled composition, preservation, and independent verification.

The objective is not the proliferation of generated artifacts. It is the growth of **independent constructive capacity**.

This is an open research program. Its outcomes must be earned through evidence rather than asserted through the fluency of a model or the ambition of a design.
