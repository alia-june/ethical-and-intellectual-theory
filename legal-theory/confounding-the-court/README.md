# Confounding the Court

**Open-World Risk, Transformative Observation, Adversarial Capture, and Defensive Reflexive Inversion**

[Read the research synthesis (PDF, pages 1-14)](confounding-the-court-pages-01-14.pdf).

This research examines how a decision-making institution can be redirected through the evidence, procedures, classifications, and apparent improvements it uses to pursue its own purposes. Its legal application treats a court as both an epistemic institution and a source of enforceable authority: a captured process can lend legitimacy and force to a substituted objective.

The synthesis connects open-world uncertainty, the transformations involved in observation, adversarially selected inputs, corrupted recovery baselines, and purpose capture. It develops **confounding the court** and **lawful-means confounding** as proposed analytical concepts, then considers **defensive reflexive inversion**: containing an attempted capture while preserving evidence for independent review and attribution.
